# Lift

*A visual philosophy for the Wingman mark — a graphite & cobalt world.*

## The movement

**Lift** is the aesthetics of the instant before flight — potential energy made visible. It rejects the literal bird and the literal letter alike. Where a logogram explains, Lift *moves*. A single wing, caught mid-upstroke, carries the whole idea: assistance that arrives under you and pushes upward. The mark is not a picture of a wing; it is the *gesture* of one — the swept geometry of a primary feather group, abstracted until only the lift remains.

## Form and space

The wing is built from layered primary feathers, each a tapered blade with a convex leading edge and a concave trailing edge, overlapping like a folded hand opening. Feathers shorten and steepen toward the wrist, lengthen and flatten toward the tip — the natural cascade that the eye reads as *wing* before the mind names it. Negative space between blades is as deliberate as the blades themselves: thin graphite channels that let the form breathe and keep it legible when it collapses to sixteen pixels. The composition sweeps from the lower-left wrist to the upper-right tip on a diagonal of ascent — never horizontal, never falling.

## Color and material

Two values only: graphite ground, cobalt feather. No gradient, no third hue, no decorative glint. The restraint is the craft — a single confident blue shape on near-black, calibrated so the contrast survives a toolbar of gray icons. The inverted state (cobalt ground, pale feather) is the same geometry, not a different idea. The palette is fixed; the discipline is in the drawing.

## Scale and rhythm

The mark is designed at its smallest use first — the favicon — and scales up without adding a single detail. Every feather edge is weighted so that at sixteen pixels the wing still reads as feathers, not a blur. This is the painstaking part: each blade tuned so the gaps never close and the tip never dulls. The rhythm is the cascade of feather lengths — a measured decrescendo that feels effortless precisely because it was labored over.

## Craft

The final wing must look like the product of countless hours by someone at the top of the field — the kind of curve that only resolves after fifty rejected versions. No stripe stands in for a feather; no arc is left where a considered Bézier belongs. Master-level execution means the mark feels inevitable, as if it could not have been drawn any other way. Meticulously crafted, quiet, and certain — a wing that earns the name Wingman.
